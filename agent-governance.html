6,000 AI agents, one company — and nobody was tracking them until it was almost too late.

An insurer with 3,500 employees discovered its people had built 6,000 AI helpers with access to company data. Your shop's number is smaller. Your visibility is probably worse. Here's the honest playbook.

The 6,000-agent story

On September 24, 2026, specialist insurer Hiscox said its roughly 3,500 employees had collectively built more than 6,000 AI agents with Microsoft Copilot and Copilot Studio — and that it is now introducing Microsoft's Agent 365 specifically to track what was built, spot duplicate agents, and flag the risky ones. Hiscox calls its approach "Citizen AI": underwriters, claims people, finance and HR staff building small agents that don't just assist with tasks but complete them. It worked — one chief underwriting officer's "Tone of Voice" agent saved him six or seven hours preparing two board reports.

Here's the part that travels: those agents read company data with the permissions of whoever built them. Hiscox is a company with a data culture lead and an AI program, and they still needed a control plane after the fact to find everything. If trained people at an insurer built 6,000 agents before governance caught up, the honest question for your shop is simpler: do you know what AI helpers your people have already built that can read your files, your email, your client data?

And the agents themselves are under attack — weekly now. Attacks on agents aren't a future worry; September coverage reported an OAuth flaw in the MCP Python SDK that could let malicious servers hijack AI agent accounts. The helper your staff built to read their inbox can be turned into someone else's inbox reader. Ungoverned agents aren't just a mess — they're an attack surface.

What Microsoft's answer looks like — and the honest small-shop path

Microsoft's answer is Agent 365, a central registry and policy engine for AI agents — every agent gets an identity, a named owner, a business purpose, and an access scope, using the same identity and security primitives IT already uses for people. At $15 per user per month standalone, it's real money for a small shop, and it's heaviest for Microsoft-licensed environments.

The good news: agent-governance tooling is shipping weekly, including SMB-tier. On September 29, 2026, Codenotary launched AgentMon Start — AI-agent monitoring built for small and mid-sized businesses at $15 per user per month, explicitly "without requiring a dedicated security team." You don't have to buy the enterprise version to start governing.

The honest version of this page's advice doesn't require buying anything this week:

  • Inventory first. Check the Copilot Studio admin center and ask your team, in one conversation, which AI agents or helpers they're using that touch company data.
  • Write the one-page policy. The 2026 edition of the SMB1001 small-business cybersecurity standard now requires a formal acceptable-use policy for AI tools. Borrow the requirement even if you never pursue certification.
  • Block what you can't account for. An agent with no identifiable owner and access to company data gets suspended until someone claims it. That's governance, and it costs nothing.

Five actions — the owner checklist

1. Ask the one question, this week

Ask every employee: "What AI tools or agents are you using that can read our company files or email?" One conversation, no blame attached. You can't govern what you don't know exists.

2. Inventory the admin centers

Open the Copilot Studio / Power Platform admin center and list every published agent. Anything with no clear owner gets flagged.

3. Write one page of policy

Write down what company data AI agents are allowed to touch, and who approves new ones. (SMB1001:2026 requires this as a formal control.)

4. Suspend the orphans

Agents nobody claims get turned off until someone does. Orphaned agents with data access are exactly the kind attackers hijack.

5. Price the fix for your environment

If you're on Copilot licensing, review Agent 365's registry; if your team runs coding/dev agents, price SMB-tier monitoring. Governance is a line item, not a project — get the number, then decide.
