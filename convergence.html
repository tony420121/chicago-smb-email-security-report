One Tuesday, five deadlines: October 13 is Patch Tuesday AND the day your software stops getting security updates.

Five things happen to your computers on the morning of Tuesday, October 13, 2026 — and the fix for your broken backups lands in the same bundle. That convergence is worth a one-page plan — so here's the plan.

What's converging on October 13, 2026

October 13 is Patch Tuesday — Microsoft's monthly security-update day. It is also, on the same morning, the end-of-the-line date for five products still common in small shops:

Office 2021

Security updates end: Oct 13, 2026

Perpetual-license Office loses security fixes. The apps keep running — but new vulnerabilities don't get patched.

Windows 11, version 24H2 (Home/Pro)

Servicing ends: Oct 13, 2026

This build stops receiving monthly security updates. Machines on it need to move to a serviced version.

Windows 10 Enterprise LTSB 2016

Extended support ends: Oct 13, 2026

The long-term branch for special-purpose machines (kiosks, shop-floor PCs) reaches end of updates.

…during the October patch wave

Patch Tuesday: Oct 13, 2026

September's patch release was the largest on record — 974 fixes dropped in a single morning (Infosecurity Magazine) — so attackers got fresh flaws to exploit while un-updated machines stood still. October's wave lands the same morning your updates stop.

Publisher 365 stops working. Completely.

Stops working: Oct 13, 2026

This one is different — not end of support, end of function. Flyers, menus, mailers, and mail-merge .pub files stop opening. Export what matters to PDF now, while it still runs.

Microsoft 365 apps freeze on Windows Server 2022

Feature freeze: Oct 13, 2026

Apps stop advancing past Version 2608. Security updates continue to October 2028, so this isn't an emergency — but freezes become emergencies two years later. Note it, plan on your timeline.

…and the backup fix you've been waiting for

Fix lands: Oct 13, 2026

Why this matters to you, in plain English: a computer that no longer receives security updates is a computer that gets easier to hack every month — and attackers know exactly which products just hit end-of-support. October 13 is the day several of your machines, potentially, step onto that list.

Two-week pre-deadline checklist (start now)

You have roughly three weeks. Here's what to do, in order:

  1. Inventory the fleet. List every PC and which build it runs (Settings → System → About → Version). Flag anything on Windows 11 24H2, Windows 10 LTSB 2016, or Office 2021. You can't fix what you haven't counted.
  2. Find the Publisher files that matter. If anyone in the shop makes flyers, menus, mailers, or newsletters in Publisher, export them to PDF before October 13 — after that, .pub files stop opening entirely. Pick the replacement (Word templates, Canva, whatever fits) before the deadline.
  3. Check your update status. Confirm every machine is current on patches — and that updates are actually installing, not just "checking." A machine that hasn't taken a patch in months is a separate problem.
  4. Plan the moves. Windows 11 24H2 machines → update to a serviced version (25H2 or later). Windows 10 LTSB 2016 machines → upgrade to Windows 11 or replace the hardware. Office 2021 → move to Microsoft 365 apps or accept the no-patches reality with extra scrutiny.
  5. Back up before you touch anything. A verified backup of each machine's data before any OS or Office migration. Fifteen minutes of backup beats a week of "where did the client files go."
  6. Check File History before you trust it. September's updates silently broke File History backups on some machines — the fix lands October 13, but that leaves weeks of unprotected time. Open Settings → Accounts → Windows backup on each machine and confirm the last successful backup date. How to check, and your options →
  7. Mitigate what you can't migrate. Any machine that will miss the deadline gets: endpoint protection verified current, local admin rights removed from the daily user account, and its internet exposure reduced (no remote access, no direct email attachments opening). Buy time, don't borrow it.
  8. Tell your people. A 30-second note to staff: "We're updating computers over the next two weeks — save your work, expect a restart prompt." Surprise restarts are how updates get blocked.

The good news: this is a scheduled, known event — not an emergency. Owners who handle it before October 13 pay a few hours of update time. Owners who don't inherit an ever-growing vulnerability list, and attackers read that list too.
