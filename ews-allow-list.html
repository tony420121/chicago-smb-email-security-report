On October 10, your email apps need to be on the list.

Microsoft's Message Center (MC1485116): starting October 10, 2026, for Worldwide tenants, Exchange Online stops letting applications talk to your mail just because EWS is enabled. Every app needs its AppID on an explicit allow list — EWSAllowedAppIDs — or it fails silently.

What changes on October 10

Since October 1, Microsoft has been flipping tenants' EwsEnabled from $null to $false (our earlier briefing). October 10 is the second stage: having EWS enabled is no longer enough. Each application needs its AppID on the EWSAllowedAppIDs allow list. Anything not on the list doesn't get an error message — it gets silent connection failures. Your backup just… doesn't run.

Scope, stated exactly: enforcement lands October 10 for Worldwide tenants. Government clouds (GCC, GCC High, DoD) follow on their own timeline into July 2027.

The catch in the auto-population

On October 8–9, Microsoft pre-fills the list from 60 days of observed EWS activity for qualifying tenants. That covers your daily drivers. It does not cover the app that syncs once a month, the migration tool you run quarterly, or the backup job on its own schedule. Infrequently used apps get left off — and adding them is your job.

The tools that typically need the list: Veeam email backups, CRM Outlook sync, mailbox-migration tools — the same cast from our EWS briefing. If it touches Exchange through EWS, it needs an AppID on the list.

Why "silent" is the scary word: nothing errors out where you'd see it. The symptom is absence — the backup that didn't run, the sync that didn't sync. You find out when you need the backup.

The honest version

This page sells the allow-list check, not EWS education — our October 1 briefing already did the education. And the framing matters: this is an enforcement date for Worldwide tenants, not a global kill switch. If you're on a government-cloud timeline, your date is later — but the check is the same, so do it anyway.

The good news: this is a list, not a migration. Reviewing it and adding missing AppIDs is an afternoon's admin work — if you do it before October 10.

Four actions — before October 10

1. Look at the current allow list now

In Exchange Online, check what's on the EWSAllowedAppIDs list today. If it's empty or you've never looked at it, that's your answer about how ready you are.

2. List your infrequent apps

The monthly sync, the quarterly migration tool, the backup on its own schedule — write them down. These are exactly the apps the October 8–9 auto-population will miss.

3. Add the missing AppIDs manually

Get the AppID from each vendor (ask them point-blank: "what's our AppID for the EWS allow list?") and add it before October 10. Vendors that are ready have this answer; vendors that don't are telling you something.

4. After October 10: suspect the list first

Backup didn't run? Sync went quiet? Before you troubleshoot anything else, check the allow list. Silent failures are the signature symptom.
