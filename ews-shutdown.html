Microsoft started turning off EWS today — your email backup may be the first to notice.

Starting today, Microsoft is flipping Exchange Online tenants' EWSEnabled from $null to $false — tenant by tenant, not all at once. The plumbing behind your email backups, CRM Outlook sync, and mailbox migrations. Final shutdown: April 1, 2027.

What EWS is — and what's changing

Exchange Web Services (EWS) is the mail-protocol plumbing behind a surprising number of owner-visible things: email backups, CRM Outlook sync, and mailbox migrations. You don't need to understand the protocol. You need to know which of your tools depend on it — because starting October 1, 2026, Microsoft is flipping tenants' EwsEnabled setting from $null to $false — rolling out starting today, tenant by tenant. Not all at once. The off-switch is moving through the tenant list now.

What breaks, in plain English

  • Email backups that use EWS — including Veeam (see Veeam KB4820). When this flips on you, the first symptom is "the backup that was working yesterday isn't today."
  • CRM Outlook sync — your CRM's connection to Outlook mail.
  • Mailbox-migration tools — anything moving mailboxes through EWS.
  • Some third-party mail clients — check which clients your staff actually use.

Two more details that came with the enforcement start: the AppID allow list (EWSAllowedAppIDs) is now live — vendors that are ready have a path to keep working — and the Kiosk/F1/F3 license restriction takes effect, so the cheapest licenses lose EWS access as part of the same move.

Why your shop should care — the honest version

The load-bearing framing on this page: a tenant-by-tenant rollout starting October 1 is not a single-day kill. Your tenant may flip this week or next month — which is exactly why this page leads with what to check rather than when to panic.

The recovery path, if you're caught out: set EwsEnabled back to $true — roughly a one-hour recovery — but expect a service interruption while it applies. It's a stopgap, not a plan. Write this down now, while nothing is broken.

Also worth saying plainly: EWS is admin-territory plumbing. This page leads on owner-visible consequences — backups that stop, CRM sync that breaks — not the protocol. The clock is April 1, 2027: every EWS-dependent workflow needs its replacement working before then.

Four actions — do these this month

1. Ask your backup vendor

If you use Veeam (or any third-party email backup), ask them point-blank: does our backup depend on EWS, and what's your migration path? Veeam KB4820 is their own write-up — make them walk you through it.

2. List what syncs with Outlook

Your CRM's Outlook sync, any mailbox-migration tool, third-party mail clients — write the list. Every item on it is either migrated by April 2027 or broken after.

3. Know the recovery path

If something breaks before you've migrated: setting EwsEnabled back to $true recovers it in about an hour (expect a service interruption). Write this down now, while nothing is broken.

4. Put April 1, 2027 on a calendar

Permanent shutdown. Whatever depends on EWS needs its replacement working before then. That's six months — which is less time than vendor migrations usually take.
