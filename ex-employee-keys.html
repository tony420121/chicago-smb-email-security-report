Check 1 — Stale user accounts (3 min)

In the Microsoft Entra admin center: Identity → Users. Sort by last sign-in date. Anyone who hasn't signed in for 90+ days is a question mark. Former employees whose accounts are still enabled are the headline risk. Disable them now — deletion can wait (deleted accounts sit recoverable for 30 days, so disabling first is the safe move).

Check 2 — Lingering guest accounts (2 min)

Same place, filter for Guest user type. Guests were invited for one project, one vendor, one meeting — and never removed. If the project ended, remove the guest.

Check 3 — Active sharing links (2 min)

In the SharePoint admin center, review sharing links across your sites. Kill every "anyone with the link" link you can't explain. Those links bypass your user list entirely.

Check 4 — Mailbox forwarding rules (2 min)

In the Exchange admin center: check mail flow rules and individual mailbox forwarding. Look for auto-forwarding to external addresses — a classic exfiltration setup, and also a classic "former employee still getting the invoices" setup. Remove anything you didn't authorize.

Check 5 — Who has admin roles (1 min)

In the Microsoft Entra admin center: Identity → Roles and administrators. Count your Global Administrators. If the number is bigger than two or three — or if it includes anyone who left — fix it today. Admin roles are the keys to the whole building.

Check 6 — Forgotten service accounts (2 min)

Accounts that aren't people: scanner@, backup@, vendor integration accounts, the account someone made for a one-time migration in 2022. Each one needs a named owner, a rotated password, and MFA — or it needs to be disabled. Attackers probed 5,700+ Microsoft 365 accounts in September 2026 and broke into exactly the forgotten-service-account kind.

Check 7 — Blocked sign-in for the departed (1 min)

For every former employee: confirm sign-in is blocked, licenses are reclaimed, and their mailbox is either converted to shared or deleted per your retention policy. A disabled account with an active mailbox and an old phone still receiving MFA prompts is a half-closed door.
