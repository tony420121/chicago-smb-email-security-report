A phishing kit that switches off your strongest login before you use it.

BigBear 2.0 didn't just bypass MFA at 258 organizations. It actively disabled FIDO2 to force victims onto weaker login methods. They didn't pick the lock — they lowered the bar, then walked over it.

The downgrade

What makes this a different beast from every other MFA story you've heard: BigBear 2.0 actively disabled FIDO2 — the phishing-resistant login method — to force victims onto weaker login methods. Most MFA attacks steal your session or trick you into approving a prompt. BigBear added a step: it switched off the victim's FIDO2 enrollment, so the fallback was SMS or an app approval — methods an attacker can intercept or fatigue. The account still had FIDO2 in theory. In practice, the strongest login was switched off before the victim ever used it.

Why your shop should care — the honest version

This is the downgrade beat, not the theft beat. Our other pages cover session theft (why MFA alone isn't enough) and the phishing page that steals your password outright (the screen that steals your password). This one is about the bar being lowered — a different failure, and a different fix.

Here's the uncomfortable part: downgrade attacks work because most shops treat "we have MFA" as a single checkbox, not an ordered stack. The enrollment order — which method gets enrolled first, which fallbacks exist — is the thing BigBear exploited. Nobody audited that, because nobody thought of it as an attack surface. Your FIDO2 enrollment is only as strong as the weakest fallback sitting next to it.

Three actions — do these this month

1. Enroll the strong method first

Register FIDO2 or a passkey before any SMS or voice fallback exists on the account. The strongest method should be the default, not the spare. If a user enrolls SMS first and FIDO2 later, the fallback ladder is backwards.

2. Audit your auth methods for downgrades

Review accounts for weaker fallbacks (SMS, voice) that sit next to a phishing-resistant method. If FIDO2 is on and SMS is also on, the downgrade path is open. The question isn't "do we have the strong method" — it's "can the strong method be switched off?"

3. Restrict enrollment itself

Limit who can add or change authentication methods in your Microsoft 365 / Entra admin settings. The downgrade is an admin setting being abused — treat it like one, not like a user preference.
