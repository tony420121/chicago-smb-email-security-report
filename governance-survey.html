Three in four organizations had a governance incident this year — and 38% left former employees with the keys.

On September 23, 2026, ShareGate released its second annual State of M365 report: 77% of organizations experienced at least one Microsoft 365 governance incident in the past year. And now AI tools — Copilot deployment doubled in a year — are reading across all of that messy, overshared content.

The numbers

The report draws on two surveys of nearly 1,800 IT professionals and leaders across nine countries. In the past year:

77%
experienced at least one Microsoft 365 governance incident
38%
left former employees or guests with access they should have lost
35%
hit an audit or compliance gap
  • 26% had sensitive content reach the wrong people
  • 67% now run two or more AI tools on that same content (28% run three or more)
  • Full Copilot deployment roughly doubled — from 29% to 56% — in a year
  • Only 1% use a purpose-built governance tool; 57% use built-in Microsoft tools, 38% rely on manual or internal policies, 4% have no governance at all

Source: PR Newswire, Sept 23, 2026 — ShareGate's second annual State of M365 report: "Three in four organizations experienced Microsoft 365 governance incidents in the past year, as AI adoption outpaces the skills to govern it" — prnewswire.com

Why it matters more now than a year ago

ShareGate's own framing: "AI Adoption moved. Controls did not." Content that was once merely overshared is now a liability an AI tool can retrieve on request. The ex-employee who still has access isn't just a file-permission problem anymore — in a Copilot-enabled tenant, they're one prompt away from your payroll spreadsheet. The 38% stat isn't a governance statistic. It's an attack surface.

The honest caveat

ShareGate sells governance tooling — this is a vendor-funded survey, not independent research. Treat the numbers as directionally useful, credited exactly as published, not gospel. That said: the direction matches everything else in this report's evidence pile — forgotten accounts get attacked (see the TeamFiltration story), and AI adoption is outpacing the controls.

The 5-step cleanup starter

1. Kill the ghosts first

Former employees and old guests — the 38%. Disable every account that belongs to someone who doesn't work there anymore, this week.

2. Audit your sharing links

In the SharePoint admin center, find every "anyone with the link" link in your tenant. Most of them were created for one email and never expired.

3. Check who can see what AI sees

If Copilot or another AI tool touches your tenant's content, its answers are only as clean as your permissions. Overshared files become AI-retrievable answers.

4. Write down who owns access reviews

One named person, one calendar reminder, once a quarter: "who still has access to what, and should they?"

5. Close the audit gaps before an auditor finds them

The 35% who hit a compliance gap didn't plan to — they just never looked.
