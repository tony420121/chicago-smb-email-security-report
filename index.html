What it means for your business

Unauthenticated email isn't a tech footnote. It's an open door with your name on it.

Attackers don't hack inboxes anymore — they borrow your identity. When your domain has no DMARC enforcement, anyone can send email that says it's from you, and most mail servers will deliver it without a second glance. Here's what that looks like in practice:

Invoice fraud aimed at your customers

A spoofed email "from" your business sends a customer a "corrected" invoice with new payment details. They pay the attacker — and blame you when the money's gone. For trades and professional services, where five-figure invoices are normal, one hit can end a client relationship permanently.

Business email compromise (BEC) aimed at your own staff

"Hey, this is urgent — wire the vendor payment today." Sent from your domain, to your bookkeeper, with your signature block copied from your website. BEC costs U.S. businesses billions a year because it exploits trust, not technology — and a missing DMARC record is what makes the forgery deliverable.

Phishing that burns the reputation you spent years building

Your customers get a "payment declined" or "verify your account" email wearing your logo and your domain. Even the ones who don't click it now associate your name with a scam. Reputation damage doesn't show up on a balance sheet until it does — all at once.

The uncomfortable math: with 81.5% of scanned Chicago SMB domains lacking DMARC enforcement, your business probably looks exactly like the easy targets attackers prefer. The good news? Fixing it is a one-time configuration — not a product, not a subscription, and not a project.

Methodology & notes

How this report was built

The scan

On 2026-09-16, 410 Chicago small-business domains across six verticals (including dental/medical, trades, and restaurants) were checked using public DNS-over-HTTPS lookups only: TXT records at the domain apex, DMARC records at _dmarc, a fixed set of common DKIM selectors, MX records, and MTA-STS policy records. No websites were visited, no mail was sent, no inboxes were touched — only the same public DNS data any mail server on earth can already see.

What the numbers mean

Results are reported as aggregates only. No business names were recorded in the scan dataset and none are published anywhere in this report. A "red/yellow/green" score reflects the state of public DNS records on the scan date — it does not measure internal security practices, endpoint protection, or employee training.

Opt-out & re-check

Because only aggregates are published, no individual business can be identified from this report. If you believe your domain's posture has changed since 2026-09-16 — for example, you've since moved DMARC to enforcement — run the free checker for a current, live read of your own domain. A note on timing

Email authentication is a snapshot discipline: records can change the day after a scan. Treat 2026-09-16 as the "photo date" for every statistic on this page, and treat the free checker — not this page — as the current answer for your domain.
