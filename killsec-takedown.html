110 terabytes of stolen data, now in police hands.

Operation KillSwitch — a German-led, Europol/Eurojust-coordinated takedown — just seized the KillSec ransomware group's entire leak-site business. The gang that would have published your data no longer exists.

What happened

Operation KillSwitch seized KillSec's whole operation: 110 terabytes of stolen data, 5 servers, 5 domains, three arrests. That collapses the group's double-extortion leverage — the entire ransomware business model for crews like this is "pay us or we publish your data." When the police hold the data, the servers, and the domains, there is nothing left to pay for.

The scale, as the outlets report it: Eurojust and The Hacker News cite roughly 1,000 suspected attacks worldwide; CyberScoop puts confirmed successful compromises at about 500 since 2024. Those are two different outlets' figures and they have not been reconciled publicly — evidence the crew was busy, not a precise census.

The scary second beat: the alleged boss is 16 years old, and the crew used AI to build its ransomware infrastructure and pick its targets. Ransomware is no longer a criminal syndicate in a faraway office. It's a teenager with a chatbot — and that is exactly why shops your size are in the blast radius. The entry bar didn't just lower. It collapsed.

Why paying never works — now with proof

Every takedown like this one is the don't-pay doctrine made visible: the money doesn't buy your data back, it funds the next crew. This time the crew got raided instead. If you got a ransom note from KillSec: their publish leverage is gone — but confirm with your IT/security contact before you exhale, and keep the note and any correspondence.

Why your shop should care — the honest version

This is a good-news story, and good-news stories are dangerous for a security page — the "we're safe now" reading undercuts everything. So this page doesn't do the victory lap. It sells the entry-bar-collapsed lesson: the defenses that stop a 16-year-old with AI stop everyone else too.

KillSec is gone; the next crew is already recruiting. The takedown is proof that the model is fragile — and proof that your shop was never too small to be on somebody's target list.
