Your Mac’s Mail just stopped syncing — 3 ways to get it back.

On October 1, Microsoft started turning off Exchange Web Services by default — and Apple Mail and Calendar on Mac still sync your work email through EWS. Apple's Graph fix hasn't shipped. Your iPhone and iPad are unaffected.

Why your Mac broke

On October 1, 2026, Microsoft began disabling Exchange Web Services (EWS) by default in Exchange Online — tenant by tenant, not all at once. Here is the part that hits your desk: Apple Mail and Calendar on Mac still sync Microsoft 365 work accounts through EWS. Apple says it is "actively working with Microsoft" to move Mail, Calendar, Contacts, Notes, and Reminders to the Graph API in a future macOS 27 update — but it has not shipped (the 27.1 betas skipped it; the 27.2 betas mention nothing). Your iPhone and iPad sync through Exchange ActiveSync, a different path — they are unaffected.

The three ways back

  • Ask your IT admin whether EWS was disabled for your account. If yes, they can re-enable it — roughly an hour's recovery.
  • Switch to Outlook for Mac meanwhile. It does not use EWS, so it keeps working while the admin sorts the Mail app.
  • Don't wait on Apple's Graph update. It is promised, not shipped, with no date. The fixes above work today.

Second EWS check — October 10 and after: starting October 10, EwsEnabled=$true alone no longer grants an app access. Every app needs its AppID on the EWSAllowedAppIDs allow list. If your mail tools break again after the 10th, the AppID list — not just EwsEnabled — is the new suspect. Your IT admin's fix: add the app's AppID to the list.
