Microsoft's update turned off your Office this week.

On September 28, 2026, Microsoft paused the rollout of update KB5002907 after reports that it deactivated — or in some cases completely removed — perpetual Office 2016 and Office 2019 installations. People opened Word and found an "enter your product key" prompt where a working app used to be. Some found no app at all.

What the update was supposed to do

KB5002907 — "Optional update for out-of-date Microsoft 365 Apps installations" — was a repair update for Windows PCs running Microsoft 365 Apps that were more than 90 days behind on updates, on Current Channel or Monthly Enterprise Channel. Delivered through Windows Update, and Microsoft says it does not install automatically. (BleepingComputer; Techstrong IT)

Who actually got hit — the honest version

The reports didn't come from Microsoft 365 Apps users. They came from perpetual-license Office 2016 and Office 2019 — the one-time-purchase versions, not subscriptions. On September 24, a Belgian MSP told BleepingComputer that five unrelated customers called within about ten minutes, dozens of PCs affected: Office suddenly showing "Unlicensed Product," a smaller number with Office gone entirely. Re-entering the original product key restored activation. Be honest about scope: this hit holdout devices that received the repair update — not everyone's Office.

Microsoft's word
A Microsoft spokesperson told The Register: "We are aware of these reports and have paused the update while we investigate." The support bulletin now reads: "As part of the repair action applied by this update, some devices were left without functioning versions of Microsoft 365 Apps or Office." And Microsoft's guidance is explicit: buying a new subscription is not a required recovery step. (The Register, Sept 28, 2026; AiCybr)

Two fixes — do these now

1. The 10-minute fix

Re-enter the original Office product key, or sign into the Microsoft account tied to the purchase and reselect the license. That restored activation in most reported cases. (BleepingComputer)

2. The hour-long fix

If Office is gone, reinstall the purchased Office release from the Microsoft account associated with the purchase — then sign out and back in. (tech-ish, Sept 27, 2026)

3. Inventory the fleet

Which machines run Office 2016/2019, which run Office 2021, which run Microsoft 365 Apps — and check installed updates for KB5002907 so you know whether it landed. You can't plan what you haven't counted.

The bigger deadline behind this week's scare

Here's the part that matters more than the pause: support for Office 2016 and 2019 ended October 14, 2025 — they're running on borrowed time already. And on October 13, 2026, Office 2021 loses security updates too — the same Tuesday Windows 11 24H2 and Windows 10 LTSB 2016 stop getting patched. This week's deactivation scare is the preview; October 13 is the real deadline for holdouts. See the convergence one-pager.
