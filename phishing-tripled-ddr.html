Microsoft says the break-in rate tripled.

Microsoft published its 2026 Digital Defense Report on October 1 — covering July 2025 through June 2026 — and the numbers are blunt: phishing as the way in jumped from 7% to 23% of Microsoft's own incident-response cases. The platform vendor is telling you the break-in rate tripled.

The honest version

Survey stats are the highest-converting evidence this report carries — and these come from Microsoft itself, which no vendor survey beats on authority. But a survey doesn't fix anything. Three things to do before Monday

1. Move to phishing-resistant MFA where it counts

AiTM kits at 44.6% exist to beat texted codes and push approvals — they relay your real login in real time. For email, VPN, and banking: passkeys or hardware security keys. A code you can be tricked into handing over isn't a second factor anymore; it's a speed bump.

2. Patch like the window is a day, because it is

Median discovery-to-weaponization is under 24 hours. Pick one patch day each week and keep it — and turn on automatic updates on anything you don't actively manage. The machines nobody patches are the inventory.

3. Make your domain hard to impersonate

87.7% of these intrusions harvest credentials or sessions — and the fake login page only works if your domain is easy to spoof. Run the free checker below: DMARC, SPF, and DKIM set up correctly turn your domain from an open costume box into a locked door.
