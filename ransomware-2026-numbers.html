Ransomware data theft is up 275%. This is what "it's an SMB problem now" looks like in numbers.

Zscaler's ThreatLabz 2026 Ransomware Report, released September 30, 2026, says attackers exfiltrated 896.2 terabytes of data — up 275.8% year over year. The headline number is not about encryption. It's about theft: steal first, extort with the data, and let the encryption be optional.

1. 896.2 TB stolen — up 275.8% in a year

Among the top 10 ransomware groups by reported leak volume, combined exfiltration rose 275.8% year over year — more than seven times the volume recorded in the 2023–2024 reporting period (123.8 TB). When attackers can steal multiple terabytes from a single organization, the extortion pressure grows even if victim counts stay flat.

2. $328 million in ransom payments, tracked on the blockchain

The average payment rose 5.3% year over year to $431,995.

3. 62% of victims held manager-level titles or above

Ransomware groups now target "high-impact" business users — not just technical admins. Intrusions increasingly start with something routine: a support interaction, a Teams message, or an IT-themed request from a seemingly credible source. Roughly 75% of victims worked in finance, sales, operations, HR, and marketing — functions with workflow access and data proximity.

4. The tools are yours

Threat actors are increasingly abusing trusted enterprise tools — Microsoft Teams and Quick Assist named explicitly — for social engineering, lateral movement, data theft, and file encryption. This is September 2026's pattern, confirmed at ransomware scale: the delivery vehicle is trust.

5. The US is the top target

US organizations accounted for 50.7% of observed ransomware activity — far ahead of Canada (4.8%), Germany (4.3%), and the UK (4.1%). Victim volumes held nearly flat (7,366 victims listed on leak sites, down just 3% year over year), with Qilin, Akira, and INC Ransom accounting for 34% of disclosed victims.

Attribution: every number above comes from Zscaler's ThreatLabz 2026 Ransomware Report, released September 30, 2026 — their researchers' telemetry from ransomware leak sites and blockchain transaction tracking.

The honest version — read this part

Zscaler sells security products. These numbers come from their researchers' telemetry, and they're presented to make the threat legible — which also happens to make Zscaler's products look necessary. Take the numbers as directional evidence from a vendor with a viewpoint, not independent research.

Why they're still worth your attention: the pattern matches everything else from September 2026 — the CSuite and N0va operations, the Teams-abuse findings, the executive targeting. Independent events, same direction.

Backups don't save you from theft. Backups save you from encryption. When the business model is "we stole your client list," the recovery plan is a response plan — and Nationwide's September 23, 2026 survey found only 35% of small and mid-market businesses have an up-to-date incident response plan. Be in that 35%.

The theft-era five — do these this quarter

1. Test a restore, not just a backup

A backup you haven't restored is a hope, not a plan. This quarter, pick one machine and actually bring a file back.

2. Protect the managers

62% of victims are manager-level and up. Your office manager, your bookkeeper, your sales lead — they're the targets now. Phishing-resistant MFA for them first.

3. Treat Teams messages like email

If your staff would verify a suspicious email, they should verify a suspicious Teams message or Quick Assist session too. Same skepticism, new channel.

4. Write the one-page response plan

Who do you call, what do you disconnect, who talks to clients, who calls the insurer. One page. Only 35% of shops have one — be in that 35%.

5. Check the front door

Stolen sessions and impersonated domains are how theft starts. The report's scan found 81.5% of Chicago SMB domains lack DMARC enforcement. See where your domain stands.
