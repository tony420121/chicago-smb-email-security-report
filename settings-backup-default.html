26H2 turned on Settings Backup whether you asked or not.

Windows 11 26H2 flipped Settings Backup to default-on for eligible commercial devices. “Not Configured” no longer means off — user settings and Store-app lists now upload to the cloud about every 8 days. Here is the one policy to check before it contradicts your data rules.

The default changed under you

A policy you never set now behaves as if you turned it on. That's the whole story — and the whole problem. If your data rules say certain user data stays local, the update may already be contradicting them.

Who is in scope — exactly

The eligibility scope is load-bearing, so here it is straight: devices that are Entra joined or hybrid-joined, running 26H2 or later — and not in EU-DMA regions or sovereign clouds. If your fleet isn't Entra-joined, this page isn't about you.

  • What uploads: user settings and Microsoft Store app lists, about every 8 days.
  • What doesn't: restore is still off by default — an admin has to enable that separately. Don't conflate the two.
  • The one policy: the Settings Backup / "Enable Windows Backup" policy — via Intune (Settings Catalog → Administrative Templates → Windows Components → Sync your settings), Group Policy (same path), or CSP.

The compliance play: set it explicitly — on or off, your call. An auditable record of a decision beats a changed default every time an auditor asks.

Why your shop should care — the honest version

This page walks a deliberate line: it is not "Microsoft is stealing your data." It's "a default changed, and your data rules may now say something your fleet doesn't do." The eligibility scope is stated exactly because oversimplifying it would embarrass the funnel — the EU-DMA exclusions, the restore-off-by-default boundary, and the Entra-joined requirement are all load-bearing.

And it's frank about the economics: admin-hygiene beats are the weakest-converting beats on the list, but the Oct-13 patch wave is this page's natural ship buddy — your fleet already changes on the 13th — and "the default changed under you" is a concrete, checkable claim.

Four actions — the one-policy check

1. Check whether your devices are in scope

Entra joined or hybrid-joined, 26H2 or later? If yes, keep reading. If no, you're done.

2. Set the policy explicitly — on or off, your call

Intune, Group Policy, or CSP: “Enable Windows Backup.” Decide what your data rules require and set it.

3. Remember restore is separate

Backup uploading does not mean restores are enabled. If you want restores, that's a second, explicit decision.

4. Write the decision down

Date, setting, who decided. An auditable record beats a changed default — that's the whole compliance play.
