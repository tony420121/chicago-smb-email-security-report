Windows 11 26H2 went GA this week — and there's a trap for some machines.

Build 26300 is rolling out gradually. It's an enablement package, not a reimage project — and devices on 26H1 can't move to 26H2 at all. Here's what that means for your shop, and three actions.

What 26H2 is

Windows 11 26H2 reached general availability on September 29, 2026 — build 26300 — and Microsoft's release-information page now carries the 26H2 row. The rollout is gradual: Microsoft holds back machines with known issues (safeguard holds), so don't promise every device upgrades on day one.

The good news first: 26H2 deploys as an enablement package. The bits are mostly already on the machine; the package lights them up. That's a small, fast deployment — not a reimage project. And the three tools you already own — Windows Autopatch, Intune, and WSUS — already support exactly this rollout pattern. The job is rollout rings and validation, not tooling.

The 26H1 trap — read this part

A device on Windows 11 26H1 cannot move to 26H2 at all. 26H1 is a parallel track for new hardware — not a stepping stone. There is no upgrade path from 26H1 to 26H2. If any of your machines are on 26H1, they don't get 26H2. Audit your fleet for 26H1 before you tell anyone what the upgrade plan is, because no plan that ignores this is credible.

This is the single most consequential fact in this release: an owner who hears "26H2 is out" and promises staff their machines will get it is making a promise some machines can't keep. Inventory first, announcements second.

Why your shop should care — the honest version

Here's the plain part: this is endpoint lifecycle inside an email-security report — a slight lane stretch, and worth saying so. It earns its place because the October 13 convergence already owns the deadline story: Windows 11 24H2 stops getting updates on October 13. This page is the companion — the deadline is when, 26H2 is where. Your 24H2 machines need a landing spot, and 26H2 is it.

GA optimism is not a deployment plan. Release announcements always sound smooth; the real world has safeguard holds, driver edge cases, and third-party app quirks. Rollout rings and pilot validation aren't caution for its own sake — they're how you avoid turning a smooth enablement package into a Monday-morning helpdesk spike.

Three actions — do these this month

1. Find your 26H1 machines first

Before anything else, inventory your fleet for any device on 26H1 — those don't get a 26H2 path at all. Check your Intune device inventory (or ask your IT provider) for machines on 26H1. This step costs nothing and prevents the one promise you can't keep.

2. Ring the rollout

Pilot group first, then expanding waves. Validate real apps — not just the OS version number — in the pilot before widening. Don't fight the safeguard holds; plan the timeline around them, not the GA date.

3. Don't build a reimage project

It's an enablement package. If your plan looks like a full reinstall for every machine, you're over-engineering it — and your staff will feel it. Rings and validation, not weekend rebuilds.
